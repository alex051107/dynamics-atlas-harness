1. **Protein** — Receiver domain of Nitrogen Regulatory Protein C (NtrC, "NtrCR"), bacterial response regulator [paper, Introduction]. Organism *Salmonella typhimurium* (strain UK-1/ATCC 68169), gene glnG/ntrC, UniProt A0A0M3KKS6 — not in the article text, taken from the PDB 2MSL cross-reference [our reading of the paper's data].

2. **Construct and conditions** — Full receiver domain, wild type, 124 aa; no mutations used in this paper's own work [our reading of the paper's data, PDB sequence]. Apo: 1 mM U-¹³C,¹⁵N protein, 50 mM sodium phosphate, 50 mM NaCl, pH 6.75, 308 K. BeF3⁻-activated: 0.3 mM protein, 50 mM sodium phosphate, pH 6.75, 308 K [both, our reading of the paper's data, PDB records; prep cited only as "previously described," paper Methods]. 600 and 800 MHz spectrometers [paper, Methods].

3. **Apo relative to** — Phosphorylation of Asp54, transferred physiologically from histidine kinase NtrB; BeF3⁻ is used here as the stable phosphomimetic for "activated" [paper, Introduction; Methods].

4. **Verdict** — Weak: the paper states an apo two-state equilibrium with exchange rate and minor-state population, but both are cited from three prior papers (refs 11–13), not measured here; functional relevance is proposed, not tested. State identity is otherwise well supported by two new NMR structures plus cross-validated MD/MSM of both states and the transition path.

5. **Major state** — "Inactive" (I), majority apo species (~85%, i.e., 100% minus the cited 15% active) [paper, Results/Discussion], matching PDB 2MSL and MSM macrostate I. Not one fixed conformer: a heterogeneous ensemble — helix α4 span, register, orientation and secondary structure vary across the ~95% of 2,168 MSM microstates assigned to I [paper, Fig. 2F–I; Abstract].

6. **Alternative state(s)** — "Active" (A), minor apo species (~15%, cited NMR estimate) [paper, Results/Discussion]. MSM equilibrium: A≈48% of population but only ~5% of 2,168 microstates (few, similar conformers) [paper, Fig. 2A]; 15% and 48% are not reconciled. Matching structure: PDB 2MSK (BeF3⁻-activated), taken as A's structural match [inference]. Signature: α4 rotated about its axis vs I, defined backbone H-bonds, distinct Y94/Q96/Y101 rotamers [paper, Fig. 1A, 5C].

7. **Evidence type** — Exchange kinetics (rate, population) are cited from prior relaxation-dispersion-type literature, not newly measured [paper, Introduction, refs 11–13]. This paper's own new evidence: solution NMR structure of separately prepared apo and BeF3⁻ end states (chemical shifts + NOESY), plus string method, Folding@home MSM, and long unbiased MD [paper, Methods; Results].

8. **Exchange parameters** — Rate ≈13,000 s⁻¹, minor-state population ≈15% in apo [paper, refs 11–13; no new fit here]. MSM equilibrium: I≈52%, A≈48% [paper, Fig. 2]. Chemical-shift-difference (Δω) range: not stated — no CPMG/CEST fit is reported here.

9. **Residues or regions** — Helix α4 and flanking loop/strand (~residues 82–101), allosterically linked to phosphorylation site D54; side chains Y94, Q96, Y101, F99 and the T82–Y101 "T-Y coupling" contact are specifically discussed [paper, Results].

10. **Identity basis** — Two independent lines converge on the same A/I assignment: (i) NOE/chemical-shift comparison of the new apo vs BeF3⁻ structures localizes the difference to the same α4 region seen computationally (Fig. 5D–E); (ii) an independently parameterized second MSM (different force field/seeding, cited to Vanatta et al.) agrees qualitatively on what separates A from I (Fig. 6). One detail failed: the string method predicted an F99 side-chain flip that the new NOE data contradict — F99 does not move [paper, Fig. 6 discussion; Fig. 1E].

11. **Structural mapping and simulations** — Structures: PDB 2MSL (apo/I) and 2MSK (BeF3⁻/A), 25 deposited conformers each [our reading of the paper's data]. Simulations, all from this paper: two string-method pathways (CHARMM27+CMAP, 300 K) not converging to one route; a Folding@home MSM from 8,000 trajectories (~978 μs after filtering) clustered into 2,168 microstates, lumped into macrostates A/I; unbiased long Anton MD (tens of μs/state), seeded independently of the string method [paper, Results]. Simulations directly sample both states and a diverse transition-state ensemble (Fig. 3) — not an absence of sampling.

12. **Functional relevance** — Not directly tested: no binding, activity, or partner (RNA polymerase, σ54, oligomerization) assay is reported [our reading of the paper's data]. The paper proposes A's homogeneity is required to engage a specific partner, I's heterogeneity is functionally permissive, and D54 phosphorylation shifts population toward A [paper, Discussion] — proposed, not tested.

13. **Competing explanations addressed** — Force-field/seeding artifact: checked against an independent, differently seeded, different-force-field MSM, which agrees qualitatively, though the authors flag their own MSM's greater I heterogeneity may partly reflect string-pathway seeding [paper, Fig. 6]. Search bias: unbiased Anton runs begun independently of the string method reproduced the heterogeneity. Prior "T-Y coupling" mechanism (obligatory Y101–T82 coupling): addressed, not supported — new NMR data show Y101 adopts multiple orientations independent of T82 [paper, Results/Discussion].

14. **What this evidence cannot support** — No new or re-measured exchange rate or minor-state population — both are unverified literature citations here. No downstream functional consequence (transcription activation, oligomerization, partner affinity) — untested. No support for the string-method-predicted F99 rotamer change, contradicted by this paper's own NMR data. No Δω magnitude or per-residue ranking is reported. The ~48% MSM active-state population is not an observed solution population; it differs from the cited ~15% NMR value, unreconciled by the paper.

15. **Provenance** — DOI 10.1038/ncomms8284; PMC4470301. Figs. 1–6, Table 1 (NMR refinement statistics) as cited above; Supplementary Figs. 1–5 referenced in-text (pathway convergence, implied-timescale/Chapman–Kolmogorov tests, Anton/NMR comparisons), not separately opened.
