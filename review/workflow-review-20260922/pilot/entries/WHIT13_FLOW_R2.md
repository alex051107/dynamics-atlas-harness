1. **Protein** — PTP1B (protein tyrosine phosphatase 1B), Homo sapiens, catalytic nucleophile Cys215, general-acid Asp181 [paper, Introduction]. YopH, a Yersinia virulence-factor phosphatase (species not named), catalytic nucleophile Cys403, general-acid Asp356 [paper, Introduction]. UniProt: not stated [paper].

2. **Construct and conditions** — Ligand-free ("apo") enzyme vs. enzyme bound to Ac-DADEXLIP-NH2, an EGFR-derived octapeptide phosphotyrosine mimic (non-cleavable difluoromethylphosphono-Phe replaces pTyr) [paper, Results ¶6]. ¹⁵N relaxation dispersion: CPMG for PTP1B (apo and peptide-bound) and peptide-bound YopH; TROSY ¹H-R1ρ off-resonance and ¹⁵N Hahn-echo for apo YopH; 600 and 800 MHz [paper, Results ¶2,4,7]. Construct boundaries, temperature, pH, buffer: not stated in the main text (SM Methods not accessed) [paper — not stated].

3. **Apo relative to** — Phosphotyrosine-bearing substrate; operationalized as the DADEXLIP pTyr-mimic peptide [paper, Results ¶6].

4. **Verdict** — Strong. A closed WPD-loop minor state is detected by relaxation dispersion in genuinely apo enzyme for both paralogs, its population grows on peptide binding, and its shift-based identity is corroborated by independent crystal forms (1SUG, 3I80, 2I42) [our reading of the paper's data].

5. **Major state** — Open WPD loop, catalytically incompetent. Population: PTP1B 97.5±1.4%; YopH ≈97% apo (Rex-based proxy, fast exchange) [paper, Table 1; Results ¶4]. Matches "many apo PTP1B crystal structures" (ref. 22); no specific PDB ID given [paper, Results ¶5].

6. **Alternative state(s)** — Closed WPD loop, catalytically competent (general acid near the leaving-group oxygen). Apo minor population: PTP1B 2.5%; YopH ≈3% [paper, Table 1]. Becomes the major species on peptide binding (Keq falls 40→0.15 in PTP1B, 34→0.01 in YopH) [paper, Table 1]. Matches PTP1B 1SUG (apo, closed) and vanadate-bound 3I80 (PTP1B)/2I42 (YopH), per Fig. 1C; no apo-closed YopH structure cited [paper, Fig. 1 legend; our reading of RCSB metadata].

7. **Evidence type** — ¹⁵N CPMG relaxation dispersion (PTP1B apo and peptide-bound; YopH peptide-bound); TROSY-detected off-resonance ¹H-R1ρ dispersion and ¹⁵N TROSY Hahn-echo Rex (apo YopH, fast-exchange regime) [paper, Results ¶2,4,7].

8. **Exchange parameters** — PTP1B apo: kex=920±190 s⁻¹, kclose=22±5 s⁻¹, kopen=890±190 s⁻¹, Δω(¹⁵N)=2.8–3.8 ppm (W179, F182, A189). PTP1B+peptide: kex=35±5 s⁻¹, kclose=30±4 s⁻¹, kopen=4.5±1 s⁻¹, Δω(A189)=3.2±0.3 ppm. YopH apo: kex=43000±6200 s⁻¹, kclose=1240 s⁻¹ (±200 Table 1; text states ±280), kopen=42000±6000 s⁻¹, Δω(measured)=3.4–6.4 ppm (A359, S361). YopH+peptide: kex=1790±240 s⁻¹, kclose=1770±240 s⁻¹, kopen=18±2 s⁻¹, Δω=3.5–4.5 ppm [paper, Table 1].

9. **Residues or regions** — WPD loop (10 residues per paper): PTP1B W179–A189 span, analyzed W179, F182, A189 (G183, S187 too weak; V184 overlapped); YopH T358–S361, assigned all four, quantitated A359 and S361 (T358 overlapped; V360 low S/N) [paper, Results ¶2,7]. P-loop (8 residues; catalytic Cys, and Arg221 in PTP1B) shows no significant conformational change [paper, Introduction; Fig. 1 legend].

10. **Identity basis** — Per-residue Δω from the dispersion fit agrees with (a) Δω measured directly between apo (open-dominant) and peptide-bound (closed-dominant) peak positions, and (b) Δω calculated with ShiftX2 for the closed conformation from 1SUG; agreement is close for A189 (3.8/3.87/3.7 ppm), looser for F182 (3.4/3.60/4.9 ppm) [paper, Table 1, Results ¶5]. No engineered mutant independently tested the closed-state model [our reading of the paper's data].

11. **Structural mapping and simulations** — Closed loop maps to PTP1B 1SUG/3I80 and YopH 2I42 (vanadate/apo-closed forms); open loop not mapped to a specific PDB ID [paper, Fig. 1 legend; our reading of RCSB metadata]. The paper ran no MD of its own. It compares apo-YopH kex (22 µs) with prior Trp-fluorescence/T-Jump data (refs. 28–29, ns–µs) and prior MD (ref. 30, partial closure only), attributing the gap to those methods probing a faster, local Trp179 side-chain motion rather than the concerted backbone closure NMR reports [paper, Discussion ¶1].

12. **Functional relevance** — Substrate-mimic peptide binding shifts the loop equilibrium toward the closed state in both enzymes, consistent with conformational selection [paper, Table 1]. Apo kclose approximates the independently measured phosphoryl-cleavage rate kcleavage in both (PTP1B 22 vs. 30–80 s⁻¹; YopH 1240 vs. 1400–2000 s⁻¹), which the paper proposes explains the ~20–40-fold kcat difference between paralogs [paper, Results ¶2,4]. Tungstate (product mimic) dissociates at identical rates from both enzymes, arguing that the cleavage/closure step, not product release, differentiates their activity [paper, Results ¶8].

13. **Competing explanations addressed** — Individual-residue exchange rates agree with each other and the global fit, supporting one concerted loop motion over a residue-specific artifact [paper, Results ¶2]. The much faster (ns–µs) fluorescence/T-Jump timescales reported for YopH are reconciled, not contradicted, by attributing them to a different, local Trp side-chain motion rather than the backbone transition measured here; a further gap between this paper's peptide-bound YopH kinetics and prior T-Jump data under a different ligand is attributed to ligand choice but not tested directly [paper, Discussion ¶1–2].

14. **What this evidence cannot support** — That the closed apo state is structurally identical to 1SUG at atomic resolution (agreement is between shift magnitudes at 2–3 residues, not a calculated structure). A causal, within-protein test of loop closure controlling kcat (the kclose≈kcleavage link rests on comparing two natural paralogs, not a loop mutant in one protein). A directly fitted Δω or population for apo YopH (Rex-based proxy under fast exchange, more model-dependent than PTP1B's values). Any claim about a specific apo-closed YopH structure, since none is cited [our reading of the paper's data].
