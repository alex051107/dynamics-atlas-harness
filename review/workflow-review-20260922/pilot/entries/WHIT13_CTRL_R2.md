1. **Protein** — PTP1B (protein tyrosine phosphatase 1B), *Homo sapiens* [paper, main text: "PTP1B, a human phosphatase... insulin, leptin, and epidermal growth factor (EGF) signaling"]. UniProt: not stated.

2. **Construct and conditions** — Not stated: expression construct, mutations/tags, buffer, pH, temperature (Methods are in the Supplementary Materials, not retrievable) [paper — absence noted]. NMR field: 600 and 800 MHz, used for global CPMG fits [paper, main text; Fig. 4 legend]. No nucleotide/metal cofactor implicated; relevant variable is active-site peptide occupancy (field 3) [inference].

3. **Apo relative to** — Substrate. PTP1B is "apo (ligand-free)" when no phosphotyrosine ligand occupies the active site, versus bound to a non-cleavable phosphotyrosine-mimic octapeptide, Ac-DADEXLIP-NH2 (X = difluoromethylphosphono-phenylalanine, derived from EGFR) [paper, main text].

4. **Verdict** — Strong. Apo PTP1B shows CPMG exchange between a major open and minor (2.5%) closed WPD-loop state; the minor state's shifts independently match a closed apo crystal structure (via ShiftX2) and the peptide-bound state, and its closing rate matches the measured catalytic cleavage rate [paper, main text, Table 1].

5. **Major state** — Open WPD loop, apo population 97.5 ± 1.4% [paper, Table 1, "PTP1B apo" row]. Matches the open conformation of "the many apo PTP1B crystal structures" the paper cites generically (ref. 22); no single PDB ID is given for this state [paper, main text].

6. **Alternative state(s)** — Closed WPD loop, apo population 2.5% [paper, Table 1]. Matches PDB 1SUG, described by the paper as an apo PTP1B structure with closed active-site loop [paper, main text, citing ref. 23].

7. **Evidence type** — ¹⁵N-CPMG relaxation dispersion (600/800 MHz) on WPD-loop backbone amides W179, F182, A189; cross-checked against ShiftX2-calculated shifts from 1SUG and measured apo-vs-peptide-bound shift differences [paper, main text; Figs. 2A-C, 3A-B; Table 1 footnotes b-d].

8. **Exchange parameters** — Apo (global fit, F182+A189): kex = 920 ± 190 s⁻¹; kclose = 22 ± 5 s⁻¹, kopen = 890 ± 190 s⁻¹; popen/pclosed = 97.5 ± 1.4%/2.5% [paper, Table 1]. Per-residue ¹⁵N Δω (CPMG): A189 3.8 ± 0.6, F182 3.4 ± 1.3, W179 2.8 ± 0.9 ppm (W179 via Rex, not the global fit) [paper, Table 1]. Peptide-bound: kex = 35 ± 5 s⁻¹, kclose = 30 ± 4 s⁻¹, kopen = 4.5 ± 1 s⁻¹, A189 Δω = 3.2 ± 0.3 ppm [paper, Table 1]. Population thus inverts to ≈87% closed/13% open with peptide bound; kopen slows ≈200-fold while kclose is unchanged [our reading of the paper's data, Table 1].

9. **Residues or regions** — Ten-residue WPD loop, containing general acid D181; quantified residues W179, F182, A189 (G183, S187 too weak; V184 overlapped) [paper, main text]. Open-to-closed loop displacement ≈10 Å [paper, main text].

10. **Identity basis** — Three independent numbers converge per residue: CPMG-fit Δω, ShiftX2-calculated shift difference (1SUG closed vs. open prediction), and measured apo-vs-peptide-bound shift difference. These agree within error for A189 and F182; for W179 (bound resonance disappears, so direct measurement is unavailable) CPMG Δω and ShiftX2 agree within combined error [paper, main text, Table 1].

11. **Structural mapping and simulations** — Closed state: PDB 1SUG (apo PTP1B, ref. 23). PDB 3EB1 (PTP1B/small-molecule inhibitor LZP-25 [RCSB]; ref. 27) is cited as precedent that the loop can open even when occupied, not as the paper's own (uncrystallized) EGFR-peptide complex [paper, main text]. Fig. 1A also shows PDB 2I42 (YopH-vanadate) and 3I80 (PTP1B transition-state analog) [RCSB] for active-site illustration only, not state-identity evidence [paper, Fig. 1 legend]. No new simulations were run for PTP1B; MD is mentioned only for YopH, from an external prior study (ref. 30) [paper, main text].

12. **Functional relevance** — The closed WPD loop positions general acid D181 to protonate the leaving-group tyrosine oxygen during cleavage [paper, main text; Fig. 1B-D]. Apo kclose (22 ± 5 s⁻¹) approximates the independently measured kcleavage ≈ 30–80 s⁻¹ (cited kinetics, refs. 19–20) [paper, main text]. Peptide binding leaves kclose unchanged but slows kopen, shifting population toward closed — consistent with selection of a pre-existing minor conformation rather than induction of a new one [paper, main text].

13. **Competing explanations addressed** — For PTP1B, the paper states "there are no available data for comparison" from other techniques [paper, main text]. The safeguard against a spurious fit is internal: CPMG-derived Δω agrees with both a structure-based (ShiftX2) prediction and a measured shift difference [paper, Table 1]. (For YopH, studied in parallel, the authors reconcile slower NMR closing times against faster fluorescence/T-jump/MD literature values via probe specificity and differing ligands — an argument not made for PTP1B [paper, main text].)

14. **What this evidence cannot support** — Construct, tags, purification, pH, temperature and ionic strength are not stated, so results cannot be generalized beyond the (unspecified) conditions used [paper — absence noted]. The peptide is a non-cleavable mimic; no catalytic turnover was observed by NMR [paper, main text]. The kclose/kcleavage match is a rate-timescale correlation across separate experiments, not single-experiment proof that closure causes cleavage [inference]. No mutant tested the loop-closure hypothesis for PTP1B [paper — absence noted]. Shift agreement with 1SUG supports the local backbone environment at assigned residues only, not the full solution structure [inference].
