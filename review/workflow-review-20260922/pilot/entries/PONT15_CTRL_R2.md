1. **Protein** — Receiver domain of Nitrogen Regulatory Protein C (NtrCR), a bacterial two-component response regulator [paper, Abstract; Introduction ¶2]. Species not stated in the paper's text; its PDB depositions (2MSL, 2MSK) list *Salmonella typhimurium* (gene glnG/ntrC), expressed in *E. coli* [our reading of the paper's data — PDB 2MSL/2MSK]. UniProt not given in paper; PDB/SIFTS maps the sequence to A0A0M3KKS6 [our reading of the paper's data].

2. **Construct and conditions** — Full receiver domain, residues 1–124 (matches D54/T82/Y94/Q96/F99/Y101 numbering) [our reading of the paper's data — PDB sequence]. No mutations. Apo: unphosphorylated, no cofactor. "Active": BeF3⁻ (phosphomimic of phospho-Asp54) with Mg2+/F⁻ [paper, Results "NMR experiments support..." ¶1; PDB 2MSK sample_details]. Spectra at 35 °C [paper, Methods]; pH not stated in text, 6.75 per PDB [our reading of the paper's data]. 600 MHz (assignment/J-couplings), 800 MHz cryoprobe (NOESY) [paper, Methods].

3. **Apo relative to** — Phosphorylation of Asp54; operationally, absence of the phosphomimic BeF3⁻ used for the "active" sample [paper, Introduction ¶2; Results "NMR experiments..." ¶1].

4. **Verdict** — Weak. The paper reports, and structurally/computationally supports, a minor active-like conformer in apo NtrC, but the defining exchange numbers (~13,000 s⁻¹ interconversion; ~15% active population) are cited from three earlier papers (refs 11–13), not measured as new data here; this paper's new work is solution-structure NMR (NOE/CYANA) and NOESY comparison, not relaxation dispersion/CEST/ZZ-exchange [paper, Introduction ¶2; Methods].

5. **Major state** — Inactive (I), dominant apo conformer: computationally an ensemble of diverse sub-states, not one structure; central turns of helix α4 well formed, termini only ~20–30% helical; Y94 packed near α3 [paper, Results "Homogeneous active versus heterogeneous inactive state" ¶2–3; Fig. 2A,F]. MSM population ≈48% (order-paired to I, not explicitly labeled) [inference, paper Fig. 2A]. Matching structure: new NMR ensemble PDB 2MSL [paper, Accession codes].

6. **Alternative state(s)** — Active (A): narrow, homogeneous ensemble; α4 stably helical, Y94/F99 face α5, Q96 packs against α3 [paper, Results "Homogeneous..." ¶2; Fig. 1A, 2B]. NMR-estimated apo population ≈15% (cited, refs 12–13, not newly measured) [paper, Results "NMR experiments..." ¶2]. MSM population ≈52% (order-paired to A, not explicitly labeled) [inference, paper Fig. 2A]. Matching structure: PDB 2MSK (BeF3⁻), agreeing with the computational model [paper, Fig. 5B].

7. **Evidence type** — This paper: solution-NMR structure determination (assignment/NOESY/TALOS+/CYANA) of apo/BeF3⁻ forms, plus NOESY cross-peak mapping (Fig. 5E); no relaxation-dispersion/CEST/R1ρ/ZZ-exchange experiment described [paper, Methods]. Supporting computation: string-method pathways, an 8,000-trajectory (>1 ms) MSM, long unbiased MD on Anton (~21–71 μs) [paper, Results]. The cited ~13,000 s⁻¹ rate and ~15% population derive from prior NMR (refs 11–13), not this paper's experiments [paper, Introduction ¶2].

8. **Exchange parameters** — Rate ≈13,000 s⁻¹, apo (cited, refs 11–13; not measured here) [paper, Introduction ¶2]. Minor(active)-state population in apo ≈15% (cited, refs 12–13) [paper, Results "NMR experiments..." ¶2]. Chemical-shift Δ range not stated in ppm; paper gives per-residue helical propensity from shifts (Fig. 5D) and presence/absence of specific NOESY cross-peaks (Y94–α3 apo; Q96–α3 BeF3⁻) [paper, Fig. 5D,E].

9. **Residues or regions** — Helix α4 and packing partners; Methods define the region as residues 82–101. Differences concentrate at α4 termini (H84–Q96) and side chains Y94, Q96, F99, A98, plus T82/Y101 and helices α3/α5; phosphorylation site D54 [paper, Methods; Fig. 1A, 3F–G].

10. **Identity basis** — The apo minor conformer is equated with the BeF3⁻ state because (i) the new BeF3⁻ structure (2MSK) matches the computational "active" macrostate (Fig. 5B), and (ii) apo spectra show higher α4-terminus helical propensity than an inactive-only model predicts, attributed by the authors to ~15% admixture of active character (cited, refs 12–13) [paper, Results "NMR experiments..." ¶2]. No direct two-peak observation is reported.

11. **Structural mapping and simulations** — New NMR ensembles: 2MSL (apo/I), 2MSK (BeF3⁻/A) [paper, Accession codes]. Simulations sample both states and interconversion: string pathways (Str1, Str2), MSM from 8,000 Folding@home trajectories, unbiased Anton runs from each end state; some MD trajectories show full I→A and A→I transitions (Fig. 3D,E) [paper, Results].

12. **Functional relevance** — BeF3⁻ mimics phospho-Asp54, stabilizing A, which "promotes the propagation of the signal to the downstream partners" (citing refs 13,15); no new binding/transcription assay reported [paper, Introduction ¶2]. Discussion argues the well-defined A state is needed for specific partner interaction, while any non-A conformation keeps signaling off [paper, Discussion ¶1].

13. **Competing explanations addressed** — Re-examines "Y-T coupling" (Y101–T82): Y101's orientation is not tied to T82's in A, agreeing with ref 25 that Y-T coupling is not required [paper, Results "Homogeneous..." ¶2]. Reconciles its MSM (CHARMM27, string-seeded) with an independent end-state MSM (Amber99SB, ref 42): both agree on kinetic state definitions and α4-rotation but differ in inactive-state heterogeneity/F99 position, attributed to force field and an earlier F99 modeling error [paper, "Comparison of MSM models..."; Fig. 6].

14. **What this evidence cannot support** — Cannot support newly measured exchange kinetics or a directly observed two-peak minor state; the 13,000 s⁻¹ rate and 15% population are cited literature values, not new measurements. MSM populations (~52%/48%) are a force-field-dependent estimate the authors flag as possibly inaccurate versus experiment. No new mutant, binding or activity data generated here. Findings concern one protein; generalizing to other regulators is discussed, not demonstrated [paper, Results "NMR experiments..." ¶2; Discussion].

15. **Provenance** — Pontiggia et al., *Nat Commun* 2015;6:7284, DOI 10.1038/ncomms8284, PMCID PMC4470301. Figs. 1–6; Table 1.
