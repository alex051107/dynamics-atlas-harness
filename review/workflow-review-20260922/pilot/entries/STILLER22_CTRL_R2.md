1. **Protein** — Adenylate kinase (Adk), *Geobacillus stearothermophilus*. UniProt ID not stated [paper; Methods].

2. **Construct and conditions** — Full-length Adk, C-terminal TEV site + His6 tag; no mutations reported in the Adk sequence itself (the paper's point mutants — ubiquitin K6C/S20C, trigger-factor V270C — are other benchmark proteins) [paper; Methods]. Native tetra-cysteine site in the ATP lid loaded with diamagnetic Zn2+ or paramagnetic Co2+ (~20 µM) for matched PCS pairs [paper; Methods]. Samples: 2 mM enzyme, 50 mM MOPS, 50 mM NaCl, 2 mM TCEP, 0.02% NaN3, pH 7.0; three ligand conditions — "turnover" (20 mM MgCl2 + 20 mM ADP), Mg-free (20 mM ADP only), "apo" (no nucleotide) [paper; Methods; Extended Data Fig. 2]. 25 °C; single field, Agilent DD2 600 MHz [paper; Methods, "NMR spectroscopy"].

3. **Apo relative to** — Adenine nucleotide; ADP is the ligand titrated (Adk's natural substrates/product are ATP, AMP, ADP) [paper; Results, "PCS–CPMG of Adk during catalysis"].

4. **Verdict** — Not included: the only state with a solved structure and exchange parameters (the 12.6% "high-energy" state) is explicitly stated to still carry substrate/product ("the high-energy state must be occupied with substrate or product" [paper; Results]); the genuinely nucleotide-free apo sample was characterized only by static PCS/HSQC comparison, with no minor-state population, exchange rate or structure reported [paper; Extended Data Figs. 2c, 4].

5. **Major state** — Closed, Mg2+•ADP-bound conformation, ~87.4% (=100%−pB) under turnover [our reading of the paper's data; Results]. Matches closed structure PDB 4QBH, the fitting reference (Q=11.6% for the Mg2+•ADP PCS fit) [paper; Extended Data Fig. 2a].

6. **Alternative state(s)** — (i) Solved minor "high-energy" state: 12.6 ± 2.5% under saturating Mg2+•ADP; partially open but still nucleotide-occupied (AMP lid opens ~15°, ~50% toward open; ATP lid/metal shifts ~1.8 Å) [paper; Fig. 3d; Results]. Computed de novo by PCS–CPMG, no PDB ID; closer to closed (RMSD 2.67 Å to 4QBH) than open (RMSD 7.03 Å to 4AKE) [paper; same section]. Not apo. (ii) True apo sample: predominantly open ensemble, qualitatively matching 4AKE, but no sub-population, kex or structure reported — unmapped [paper; Extended Data Fig. 4a,d,f].

7. **Evidence type** — 1HN CPMG relaxation dispersion combined with pseudocontact shifts (PCS–CPMG), diamagnetic (Zn2+) vs paramagnetic (Co2+) samples; static PCS/[1H-15N]-HSQC comparison across Mg2+•ADP / ADP-only / apo [paper; Fig. 1c,f; Extended Data Figs. 2–4].

8. **Exchange parameters** — Turnover (Mg2+•ADP): kex = 1,428 ± 83 s⁻¹ (Zn2+ 1,355 ± 65; Co2+ 1,367 ± 71 s⁻¹), pB = 12.6 ± 2.5%, kopen = 180 ± 36 s⁻¹ [paper; Results; Extended Data Fig. 3]. ADP-only, Mg-free (not apo): kopen = 2.6 ± 0.3 s⁻¹, slow exchange [paper; Extended Data Fig. 3]. Per-residue Δδ range for Adk not stated (Fig. 1g maps Δδ as a color scale only). No exchange rate or population given for the nucleotide-free apo sample [paper].

9. **Residues or regions** — AMP lid (32–55): largest change; ATP lid (128–157): smaller ~1.8 Å metal shift; core = 1–28, 62–113, 165–217; hinge = 116–125 [paper; Methods; Fig. 3d]. 103 of 186 assigned residues showed an exchange (Rex) contribution under turnover [paper; Methods].

10. **Identity basis** — Not identity-by-resemblance: minor-state coordinates computed de novo from PCS restraints by maximum-likelihood/simulated-annealing rigid-body refinement (Fig. 3a,c; Supplementary Methods), then compared post hoc to known structures. Observed paramagnetic Δδ is smaller than expected from the known open (4AKE) vs closed (4QBH) difference, so the minor state is not simply the known open crystal form [paper; Results].

11. **Structural mapping and simulations** — 4QBH (closed) and 4AKE (open) used as references; new minor-state model from PCS–CPMG, top-5 likelihood structures shown (Fig. 3c), no PDB ID deposited [paper; Data availability]. No molecular-dynamics simulation of the transition is reported; the "simulated data" are synthetic PCS/CPMG benchmarks of algorithm accuracy on Adk, calmodulin and Src kinase, not new experimental findings for the latter two [paper; Results, benchmarking section].

12. **Functional relevance** — Nucleotide binds the closed major state; lid motion in the still-occupied minor state is proposed as the path for product release/substrate association via conformational selection then induced-fit closure (Fig. 3f) [paper; Results]. Product release/lid opening is cited as rate-limiting (kcat ≈ kopen ≈ 100 s⁻¹), consistent with measured kopen = 180 ± 36 s⁻¹ [paper; Results].

13. **Competing explanations addressed** — Equivalent exchange parameters from diamagnetic and paramagnetic samples support lid-opening motion over independent metal-ion exchange as the dispersion source [paper; Results]. F-statistic model comparison (slow-exchange vs "no-exchange") justified fitting at p>0.05 for the ADP-only sample [paper; Extended Data Fig. 3]. Smaller-than-expected Δδpara argues the minor state is not identical to the known open crystal structure [paper; Results].

14. **What this evidence cannot support** — Cannot support a characterized alternative apo state of Adk: the resolved minor state is nucleotide-occupied by the authors' own statement, and the true apo sample has no resolved minor-state population, kex or structure, only a qualitative bulk shift toward the open ensemble [paper; Extended Data Figs. 2c, 4]. Cannot support generalization beyond *G. stearothermophilus* Adk: the calmodulin/Src/ubiquitin/trigger-factor data are synthetic or method-validation benchmarks, not new dynamics findings for those proteins [paper; Results, Methods]. Cannot support a specific deposited atomic structure for the minor state (no PDB ID) [paper; Data availability].
