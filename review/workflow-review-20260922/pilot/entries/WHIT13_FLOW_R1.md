1. **Protein** — PTP1B, human PTP (catalytic Cys215, acid Asp181) [paper]. UniProt not given. Comparator: YopH, "a virulence factor from Yersinia" (Cys403, Asp356) [paper]; species not stated beyond genus — PDB 2I42 (cited for YopH) is annotated Y. enterocolitica [RCSB, not paper].

2. **Construct and conditions** — no domain boundaries, tags or mutations stated; PTPs use no cofactor [not stated]. NMR temperature/pH/buffer/concentration not given in main text (full Methods in Supplementary Material, not accessed) [not stated]. Fields used: 600/800 MHz (PTP1B, peptide-YopH 15N-CPMG) [paper]. Cited kcat/kcleavage (pH 6.6, 293 K, refs. 19–20) are literature values, not NMR conditions [paper].

3. **Apo relative to** — the octameric phosphotyrosine-mimic peptide Ac-DADEXLIP-NH2 (EGFR-derived; X = difluoromethylphosphono-Phe, non-cleavable), Ki "high nM, low µM" ≈ substrate Km [paper, ref. 26]. Apo = no peptide bound.

4. **Verdict** — Strong for PTP1B: a ligand-free minor closed-loop state, detected by 15N-CPMG at two fields, with quantitative rate/population/Δω for three residues, an independent closed-apo crystal structure (1SUG), and a matching shift on peptide binding [our reading, Table 1, Fig. 3]. YopH is consistent but more indirect (apo Δω not directly fit; field 8).

5. **Major state** — open WPD loop, apo-dominant, pa = 97.5 ± 1.4% (F182/A189 fit) [paper, Table 1]. Matches "the many apo PTP1B crystal structures" cited generically (ref. 22); no single PDB ID given [paper].

6. **Alternative state(s)** — closed WPD loop, minor in apo (pb = 2.5%), major when peptide-bound (~99%) [paper, Table 1]. Matches PDB 1SUG, 1.95 Å apo PTP1B with a closed loop [paper + RCSB title].

7. **Evidence type** — 15N-CPMG (600/800 MHz) on amides W179, F182, A189 (G183/S187 too weak; V184 overlapped) in apo/peptide-bound PTP1B [paper, Fig. 2A-C, 4A]. Apo YopH needed 1H-R1ρ/TROSY (motion ≥30,000 s⁻¹, too fast for CPMG) plus 15N-TROSY Hahn-echo Rex; peptide-bound YopH used 15N-CPMG [paper, Fig. 2D, 4B].

8. **Exchange parameters** (s⁻¹; Table 1 unless flagged) — PTP1B apo: kex 920±190 (F182+A189; W179 900±200), kclose 22±5, kopen 890±190, Keq 40, Δω(ppm) W179 2.8±0.9/F182 3.4±1.3/A189 3.8±0.6. PTP1B+peptide: kex 35±5, kclose 30±4, kopen 4.5±1, Keq 0.15, Δω(A189) 3.2±0.3 ppm [Fig. 4A]. YopH apo: kex 43,000±6,200, kopen 42,000±6,000, Keq 34; kclose 1,240±200 (Table 1) vs. 1,240±280 (Results text, same value) — source inconsistency, both given; Δω "Not Determined" (fast exchange); ShiftX2 Δδcalc instead: A359 3.4±0.4, S361 6.4±1.2 ppm. YopH+peptide: kex 1,790±240, kclose 1,770±240, kopen 18±2, Keq 0.01, Δω A359 3.5±0.4/S361 4.5±0.8 ppm [Fig. 4B].

9. **Residues or regions** — the 10-residue WPD loop. PTP1B assigned W179, F182, G183, V184, S187, A189; quantified W179, F182, A189. YopH assigned T358, A359, V360, S361; quantified A359, S361 (T358 overlapped, V360 low signal/noise) [paper].

10. **Identity basis** — for PTP1B, three shift-difference measures converge for A189 (fit Δω 3.8±0.6 vs. ShiftX2 Δδcalc 3.7±0.2 vs. measured Δωmeas 3.87 ppm), less tightly for F182 (3.4±1.3 vs. 4.9±1.0 vs. 3.60) [Table 1, Fig. 3A-B]. Keq inverts 40→0.15 with peptide while kclose barely moves (22→30) and kopen falls ~200-fold — consistent with binding trapping a pre-existing closed state, not inducing one [our reading]. YopH shows an analogous match, but its apo Δω was never independently fit, so identity rests more on the ShiftX2 proxy [Table 1].

11. **Structural mapping and simulations** — closed state maps to PDB 1SUG (PTP1B, ref. 23); open state maps only generically to "many apo" structures (ref. 22, no ID). PDB 2I42 (YopH–vanadate) and 3I80 (PTP1B transition-state analog, per RCSB) sit in Fig. 1A as static references, not sources of the fitted shifts. 3EB1 (PTP1B+inhibitor LZP-25 per RCSB; paper's "ligand-bound") is an exceptional bound structure with an open loop [paper]. No MD was run here; MD cited for YopH only (ref. 30) showed faster open/partly-closed transitions than the NMR closed state, attributed to differing probes, not refutation [Discussion].

12. **Functional relevance** — the closed loop positions the acid Asp to protonate the leaving-group tyrosine [Fig. 1B-D]. Apo kclose (22±5 PTP1B; ~1,240 YopH) is within ~2-fold of literature kcleavage (30–80; 1,400–2,000, ref. 20) for both enzymes; kclose barely changes on peptide binding while kopen falls sharply, supporting closure as concomitant with, not upstream of, chemistry [Results/Discussion]. The ~20-fold kcat gap (ref. 19) is proposed to track apo kclose; extra Pro residues in the PTP1B loop are an untested, "potentially" responsible cause [Discussion, speculative].

13. **Competing explanations addressed** — prior YopH fluorescence (ns–µs) and MD timescales disagree with the NMR result; attributed to those probes reporting the Trp179 side chain/partial motions vs. backbone/full closure — authors' interpretation, untested independently [Discussion]. Identity is cross-checked with three shift metrics, not one (field 10). Tungstate dissociation kinetics were identical for both enzymes, arguing against product release as the kcat-difference origin [Results, Fig. S7]. No aggregation/oligomerization test reported.

14. **What this evidence cannot support** — not direct observation of bond-breaking: the loop is "static on the timescale observable by NMR" during that step [Discussion], so kclose≈kcleavage is a rate correlation, not proof of coupling. Not a directly-fit Δω/population for apo YopH (Table 1: "Not Determined"). Not a tested structural cause for PTP1B's slower loop (Pro-residue idea is explicitly speculative). Not generalizable beyond these two PTPs; NMR conditions are unconfirmed against the cited pH-6.6/293K kinetics [not stated].
