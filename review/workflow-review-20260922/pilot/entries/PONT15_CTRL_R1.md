1. **Protein** — NtrC receiver domain (NtrC^R), N-terminal receiver domain of Nitrogen Regulatory Protein C, a bacterial response regulator [paper, Introduction]. Species: *Salmonella typhimurium* (gene glnG/ntrC/STM4005); not named in the paper's text, taken from PDB 2MSL/2MSK's organism field, cited via the paper's "Accession codes" [our reading of the paper's data]. UniProt: not stated.

2. **Construct and conditions** — 124-residue construct matching the deposited 2MSL/2MSK sequence [our reading of the paper's data]. No mutations stated; wild-type apo- and BeF3−-labeled protein, "prepared as previously described." No bound nucleotide; BeF3− gives the activated sample, linked to phosphorylation of Asp54. 35 °C; 600 MHz (assignment/J-coupling) and 800 MHz (NOESY) [paper, Methods]. pH/buffer: not stated.

3. **Apo relative to** — Phosphorylation at Asp54, relayed from histidine kinase NtrB. "Apo" = unphosphorylated, non-BeF3− sample; BeF3− is the activating surrogate used for the "activated" sample [paper, Introduction; Methods].

4. **Verdict** — Strong: apo NtrC^R reportedly samples a minor (~15%) active-like conformer alongside a dominant, heterogeneous inactive form; the minor state's identity is anchored by a new BeF3−-activated NMR structure, NOESY cross-peaks, and independent MD/Markov-State-Model (MSM) computation [paper, see fields below].

5. **Major state** — Ground "inactive" (I) apo conformation. New NMR ensemble (PDB 2MSL, BMRB 25125): helix α4 ordered only centrally, fraying at both termini; I is intrinsically heterogeneous (~95% of 2,168 MD microstates; ~48–52% by kinetic weight in the 2-macrostate MSM) [paper, Results]. No population % is given for the real major state; ~85% is the arithmetic complement of the paper's stated ~15% figure [inference].

6. **Alternative state(s)** — Minor "active" (A)-like conformer, ~15% population, a figure the paper attributes to its refs 12–13 [paper, Results, near Fig. 5D]. Matching structure: new BeF3−-activated NMR structure (PDB 2MSK, BMRB 25124), "in full agreement" with the computational active-state model [paper, Results]. Distinguished from I by axial rotation of helix α4 and repositioning of Y94/Q96 relative to helix α3 [paper, Figs 2C–D,G–H, 5C,E].

7. **Evidence type** — No new relaxation-dispersion experiment (CPMG/CEST/R1ρ/ZZ-exchange) is reported; the ~13,000 s⁻¹ rate and ~15% population are cited from the paper's refs 11–13 [paper, Introduction, Results]. This paper's own evidence: two new NMR structures, chemical-shift helical-propensity, NOESY comparison of the two samples, and MD (string method + MSM from >1 ms Folding@home, cross-checked with unbiased ANTON runs) [paper, Methods, Results].

8. **Exchange parameters** — Rate: ~13,000 s⁻¹ apo interconversion (cited, refs 11–13) [paper, Introduction]; the paper's own MSM separately computes one slow process at ~100 μs, comparable in order of magnitude [paper, Results]. Minor-state population: ~15% (cited) [paper, Results]; MSM macrostate population ~48% for A, flagged by the authors as possibly inaccurate [paper, Results]. Chemical-shift-difference range: not stated.

9. **Residues or regions** — Helix α4, ≈residues 84–101 (paper's own sub-ranges: upper 84–90, lower 90–96) [paper, Fig. 1 legend]; apo helical propensity extends to Q96 versus Y94 in older models [Fig. 5D]. Y94, Q96, F99, Y101 switch packing against helices α3/α5; loop connecting α4–β5; reference point K67; phosphorylation site D54 [paper, Figs 1A, 2C–I, 5E].

10. **Identity basis** — Minor apo state is proposed to resemble the BeF3−-activated structure because: apo helical propensity partly matches the active-like α4 extension [Fig. 5D]; the MSM "active" macrostate reproduces the same helix-rotation signature [Figs 2, 6]; NOESY cross-peaks specific to Y94–α3 (apo) versus Q96–α3 (BeF3−) track the two respective states [Fig. 5E] [paper].

11. **Structural mapping and simulations** — New NMR structures of apo/inactive (2MSL) and BeF3−/active (2MSK) solved here [paper, Methods]; earlier NMR/targeted-MD starting models (ref 22) lack a PDB ID in this paper. Simulations: two string-method free-energy pathways, an MSM from >1 ms Folding@home MD (CHARMM27), and unbiased ANTON runs, cross-checked against an independent published MSM (ref 42, Amber99SB) — all sample the active state and inactive-state heterogeneity [paper, Results, Methods].

12. **Functional relevance** — Phosphorylation of D54 "shifts" the equilibrium toward the active state, "which promotes the propagation of the signal to the downstream partners," part of transcriptional activation of nitrogen-starvation genes downstream of NtrB [paper, Introduction]. Downstream partner(s) not named in this paper; no new binding or activity assay is reported. Y101–T82 "coupling" is stated as not required for activation, per the paper's cited ref 25 [paper, Results].

13. **Competing explanations addressed** — Paper cites evidence (ref 25) against the earlier Y101–T82 "T–Y coupling" trigger model; its own MSM shows Y101 not strictly tied to T82 [paper, Results; Supp. Fig. 3A,B]. It also tests whether inactive-state heterogeneity is a CHARMM27 artifact via an independent Amber99SB MSM (ref 42) and its own ANTON runs; heterogeneity persists across force fields, while an F99 discrepancy is attributed to an error in the original NMR model, not force field [paper, Results].

14. **What this evidence cannot support** — The 13,000 s⁻¹ rate and 15% population are not new measurements here; no CPMG/CEST/R1ρ data are reported, so this is not an independent kinetic confirmation. The 52%/48% MSM populations are simulation estimates the authors flag as possibly inaccurate. BeF3− is a phospho-Asp surrogate, not authentic phosphorylation; no phosphorylated-state structure is reported. No downstream-partner or activity assay is included, so functional relevance rests on citation. PDB 2MSL is one NOE-restrained ensemble, not each heterogeneous sub-state the MSM describes.
