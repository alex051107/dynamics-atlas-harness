**1. Protein.** Abl (Abelson) tyrosine-protein kinase 1, kinase domain, from full-length Abl isoform 1b [paper, Methods]. Species Homo sapiens; UniProt P00519 (ABL1_HUMAN) [our reading of the paper's data, from PDB 6XR6 — the paper's own deposited coordinates, cited in "Data and materials availability"; not stated in the main text itself].

**2. Construct and conditions.** "Abl" = isolated kinase domain, residues 248–534, MBP-His6-TEV fusion expressed in E. coli [paper, Methods]. No bound nucleotide, substrate or inhibitor in samples used for state populations/structures (25 mM sodium phosphate pH 7.1, 75 mM NaCl, 3.0 mM BME, 100% D2O) [paper, "NMR Sample Preparation"]. Population-shifting mutants: M309L/H415P (→I1, 50%); T408Y, M309L/T408Y (→I2, 70–~100%); G269E/M309L/T408Y "I2M" (I2 ~90%, reference background) [paper]. Unliganded spectra at 10 °C (A-loop broadens ≥20 °C) [paper, Results]. pH tested 6.5–7.7 [paper, Fig. 6D–F]. Fields: Bruker 600–900 MHz and 1.1 GHz, cryoprobes [paper].

**3. Apo relative to.** ATP/nucleotide and ATP-competitive inhibitors (imatinib, PD173955, dasatinib, nilotinib, ponatinib, rebastinib, axitinib) — the kinase-domain samples used for state characterization carry none of these [paper, "unliganded Abl," Results; Methods].

**4. Verdict.** Strong — CEST exchange data fitted to an explicit 3-state kinetic/thermodynamic model, plus independent support from de novo NMR structures of both minor states (in population-shifted mutants), ITC and kinase-activity data, and chemical-shift correlation to known inhibitor-bound crystal structures; numbers traceable to Results/Fig. 1D and Methods [paper].

**5. Major state.** Ground state G: catalytically active (DFG-in, αC-in, A-loop open); population 88% [paper, Results; also given as ~88–90% elsewhere, e.g. Fig. 4D]. Structure: NMR solution structure solved in this work, PDB 6XR6 / BMRB 30770 [paper].

**6. Alternative state(s).** I1 (E1): DFG-out, αC-in, A-loop still open; population 5.9±0.1% (WT) [paper, Methods]. Structure solved via AblM309L/H415P; PDB 6XR7 / BMRB 30771. I2 (E2): DFG-out, A-loop closed and translated (~35 Å at its midpoint), αC-out, P-loop stretched; population 6.1±0.7% (WT) [paper, Methods]. Structure solved via AblM309L/T408Y / AblG269E/M309L/T408Y; PDB 6XRG / BMRB 30772.

**7. Evidence type.** 13C methyl CEST (104 assigned methyls, 87 show exchange) [paper, Fig. 1B,C]; 1H-13C–correlated spectra for quantitation once a state exceeds ~5% (slow exchange → resolved peaks) [paper, Methods]; a magnetization (ZZ-)exchange experiment on AblT408Y [paper, fig. S6C]; ITC for inhibitor affinity; western-blot kinase assays (CrkII phosphorylation) [paper, Methods].

**8. Exchange parameters.** Global 3-state fit (G⇌I1⇌I2, "model 1"): kex(G↔I1)=46.8±4.3 s⁻¹, kex(I1↔I2)=88.7±13.5 s⁻¹; pG=88%, pI1=5.9±0.1%, pI2=6.1±0.7% [paper, Methods]. CEST-detectable window: kex ~20–500 s⁻¹ [paper, Results]. Example Δϖ (13C, I2−G): Leu406 CD1≈−2.8 p.p.m., CD2≈+2.1 p.p.m.; no single overall range given [paper, Fig. 1F]. DFG-flip activation enthalpy (active→I1): ~36 kcal mol⁻¹ (Results) / ~36.4 (Fig. 6I) — paper gives both [paper].

**9. Residues or regions.** A-loop (incl. DFG motif Asp400-Phe401-Gly402, pTyr412 site), αC helix, P-loop (incl. Tyr272), regulatory spine (Met309, Leu320, His380, Phe401), gatekeeper Thr334 [paper, Fig. 1B, 4A]. 87/104 assigned methyls exchange, concentrated near A-loop/αC helix [paper, Fig. 1B].

**10. Identity basis.** CEST-derived methyl 13C shifts of I1 correlate with Abl-PD173955; I2 shifts correlate with Abl-imatinib [paper, Fig. 1E,F]. Confirmed by solving 3D structures of the population-shifted mutants and comparing to inhibitor-bound crystal structures (PD173955 complex; imatinib, PDB 1IEP) [paper, Fig. 2B–D].

**11. Structural mapping and simulations.** NMR solution structures determined for all three states (CYANA/CNS, NOE restraints, Table S1) [paper, "Structure determination"]. No MD or other simulation used; compared to existing crystal structures (imatinib complex 1IEP; axitinib complex 4TWP; AblFKΔSH3 4XEY; insulin-receptor kinase inactive state) [paper, Fig. 2D, 5D,F, fig. S12].

**12. Functional relevance.** I2 is selectively bound by imatinib (also nilotinib/ponatinib/rebastinib, fig. S4) and is stabilized by SH3-SH2 autoinhibitory docking (6%→34% in full-kinase AblFK, →95% with GNF5) [paper, Fig. 5A,B]. I1 is bound with minimal rearrangement by PD173955. pTyr412 phosphorylation depletes both I1 and I2, activating the kinase (I2M variant: pI2 90%→10%) [paper, Fig. 4F]. Imatinib-resistance mutations remote from the drug site (H415P, Y272H, E274V, F378V, G269E, Q271H) deplete I2, matching ITC-measured affinity losses [paper, Fig. 3]. Gatekeeper T334I and spine mutants (M309L, L320I) shift the G/I1/I2 balance and change CrkII-phosphorylation activity [paper, Fig. 4B,D, fig. S11].

**13. Competing explanations addressed.** Three sequential exchange topologies were compared by χ²red (model 1, G⇌I1⇌I2, =1.15 vs. 1.29 and 1.41), rather than assumed [paper, Methods]. Minor-dip assignment to I1 vs. I2 was cross-validated via opposite pH-dependence plus CEST on I2-eliminating AblH415P, not asserted from CEST alone [paper; Fig. 6D–F]. Genuine dynamic exchange (vs. two static populations) was confirmed by ZZ-exchange on AblT408Y [paper, fig. S6C]. Stabilizing mutations were checked by chemical-shift comparison to confirm population shift without a new conformation [paper, Results].

**14. What this evidence cannot support.** Populations were measured on an isolated kinase domain (248–534); I1/I2 populations (6% each) do not represent full-length cellular Abl, where the SH3-SH2 regulatory module shifts the balance (I2 rises to 34–95% in the paper's own AblFK constructs) [paper, Fig. 5A]. These constructs do not address myristoyl-cap regulation, mentioned only via citation to other studies, not the paper's own data [paper, ref. 62/69]. I1's biological significance is explicitly called "not apparent" by the authors — not evidence of physiological function [paper, Discussion]. Minor-state structures were solved in engineered mutant backgrounds, not literally in wild-type protein; the authors argue, but cannot fully exclude, mutant-specific structural perturbation beyond the intended population shift [paper, "Structure determination"].
