1. **Protein** — Abl tyrosine kinase (Abl1/c-Abl), kinase domain. Species not named in the text; construct is "cloned from full-length Abl isoform 1b" [paper, Methods]. Drugs studied (imatinib, dasatinib, PD173955) are consistent with human ABL1 [inference]. UniProt: not stated.

2. **Construct and conditions** — Isolated kinase domain, residues 248–534 ("Abl"), MBP-His6 fusion; also longer regulatory constructs "AblFK" (residues 1–534 or 83–534, includes SH3-SH2) [paper, Results/Methods]. CEST samples carried no bound nucleotide or Mg2+ ("unliganded Abl") [paper]. Buffer 25 mM sodium phosphate pH 7.1, 75 mM NaCl, 3.0 mM BME, 100% D2O, 30–300 µM protein, 10 °C (pH-dependence separately tested, 6.5–7.7) [paper, Methods]. Spectrometers 600, 700, 800, 850, 900 MHz and 1.1 GHz [paper, Methods].

3. **Apo relative to** — ATP/nucleotide and the ATP-site inhibitors imatinib, dasatinib and PD173955; the NMR samples contained neither [paper, Methods].

4. **Verdict** — Strong: two alternative states defined by quantitative CEST data, cross-validated with population-shifting mutants, structural comparison, ITC binding and activity assays, all traceable to figures [paper, Fig. 1, 3–5].

5. **Major state** — Ground state "G": A-loop open, DFG-in, αC-in, catalytically active-like; population 88% (WT, 10 °C) [paper, Fig. 1D]. Matches the active-state structure solved in this work, PDB 6XR6 [paper, data availability].

6. **Alternative state(s)** — Linear exchange G↔I1↔I2. I1: DFG-out/αC-in, otherwise close to G; population 6% (WT); shifts match the PD173955-bound crystal structure; solved as PDB 6XR7 from population-enriching mutant AblM309L/H415P (~50% I1) [paper, Fig. 1, Results]. I2: closed A-loop (middle translated ~35 Å), αC "out" (Glu305–Lys290 ion pair broken), P-loop Tyr272 shifted ~16 Å; DFG/other elements also non-productive (exact DFG rotamer not stated); population 6% (WT), 34% in AblFK (SH3-SH2 present), 95% in AblFK+GNF5; resembles imatinib-bound Abl and inactive insulin-receptor kinase; solved as PDB 6XRG from AblG269E/M309L/T408Y (~90% I2) [paper, Fig. 1, 5].

7. **Evidence type** — 13C-methyl CEST on 104 methyl probes; backbone/side-chain assignment (3D HNCACB, CBCA(CO)NH, HNCA, HNCO); NOE-based structure calculation (CYANA); magnetization-exchange NMR (peak connectivity); ITC for ligand affinity; CrkII-phosphorylation kinase assays; gel filtration for homogeneity [paper, Methods]. No CPMG relaxation dispersion reported [not stated].

8. **Exchange parameters** — kex(G↔I1) = 46.8 ± 4.3 s⁻¹; kex(I1↔I2) = 88.7 ± 13.5 s⁻¹; pG/pI1/pI2 = 88%/6%/6% (WT) [paper, Fig. 1D]. Δω given per residue, not as one summary range; example Leu406 13C Δω(CD1) ≈ −2.8 ppm, Δω(CD2) ≈ +2.1 ppm [paper, Results]. 40/104 probes show one CEST dip (I1 only), 43/104 show two dips (I1 and I2) [paper, Results].

9. **Residues or regions** — A-loop (incl. DFG Asp400-Phe401, phosphosite Tyr412), αC helix (Glu305–Lys290 ion pair, regulatory-spine residues Met309/Leu320), P-loop (Tyr272), gatekeeper Thr334; largest changes: A-loop translation ~35 Å and Tyr272 shift ~16 Å toward αC, both in I2 [paper, Fig. 2].

10. **Identity basis** — I1: measured 13C methyl chemical shifts closely match the PD173955-bound Abl crystal structure ("remarkable similarity") [paper, Results]. I2: Δω values correlate with the imatinib-bound crystal structure, and the closed-A-loop/αC-out arrangement resembles inactive insulin-receptor kinase, cited by the paper [paper, Results]. Numeric correlation coefficients not stated.

11. **Structural mapping and simulations** — Three structures from this work: 6XR6 (G, WT), 6XR7 (I1, from AblM309L/H415P), 6XRG (I2, from AblG269E/M309L/T408Y), all calculated from methyl/aromatic NOEs in CYANA [paper, Methods]. Comparator crystal structures (PD173955- and imatinib-bound Abl; inactive IRK) are cited, not re-solved here; their PDB IDs are not stated. No molecular dynamics simulations reported.

12. **Functional relevance** — G binds dasatinib with high affinity (qualitative) [paper, Results]. I1 is the conformation PD173955 binds with little extra reorganization, explaining its potency [paper]. I2, not I1, is the conformation imatinib binds [paper]. Tyr412 phosphorylation eliminates I1 and I2, stabilizing G by ~3.0 kcal/mol [paper]. Spine/DFG mutants that raise pI2 (M309L/L320I, pI2≈82%) or flip DFG (F401V) sharply lower CrkII-phosphorylation activity (F401V ≥20-fold lower) [paper, Fig. 4]. Imatinib-resistance mutations (H415P, Y272H, E274V, F378V) each deplete I2 and lower imatinib affinity ~4- to 10-fold [our reading of the paper's data].

13. **Competing explanations addressed** — Topology: global CEST fitting favored sequential G↔I1↔I2 over two branched models (χ²=1.15 vs 1.29/1.41) [paper]. Two-dip deconvolution validated using AblH415P, which depletes I2 (<1%), giving a clean two-state G↔I1 fit that fixed kex1 [paper]. Static heterogeneity vs. real exchange: magnetization-exchange NMR shows direct peak connectivity [paper]. Aggregation/impurity: mass spectrometry, NMR homogeneity checks, gel-filtration purification [paper, Methods]. Simple "DFG-out = imatinib-bound" assumption is wrong here: I1 (DFG-out) is PD173955-selective; imatinib selects the further-altered I2 [paper].

14. **What this evidence cannot support** — The paper states the biological significance of I1 "is not apparent" and considers it possibly an evolutionary by-product rather than a selected functional state [paper, Discussion]. I1/I2 structures were solved on population-enriching mutants, not WT, so fine excited-state detail in WT rests on chemical-shift inference, not direct WT observation [inference]. No simulation traces the interconversion pathway; only end conformations and bulk kex are defined [inference]. Activity data use one substrate (CrkII) and isolated/near-isolated constructs, not cellular signaling or the Bcr-Abl fusion, so results should not extend to in-cell regulation without further evidence [inference]. Populations are condition-dependent (pH 6.5–7.7 redistributes them), so the 88/6/6 split should not be treated as fixed across all solution conditions [paper/inference].
