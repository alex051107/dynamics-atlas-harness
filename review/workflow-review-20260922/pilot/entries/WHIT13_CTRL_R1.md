1. **Protein** — PTP1B: human (Homo sapiens) protein-tyrosine phosphatase 1B, regulating insulin/leptin/EGF signaling [paper, Results]. YopH: Yersinia outer protein H, "a virulence factor from Yersinia"; species not given beyond genus [paper; ref. 14]. The paper's own closed-loop comparator, PDB 2I42, is annotated Yersinia enterocolitica [data.rcsb.org], not given as the construct's source. UniProt: not stated.

2. **Construct and conditions** — Residue boundaries/mutations: not stated; SM Methods sit in a download-gated .docx, not retrieved. No cofactor/nucleotide mentioned; PTPs need none. States compared: apo (ligand-free) vs. complex with EGFR-derived octamer Ac-DADE-X-LIP-NH2 (X = difluoromethylphosphono-Phe, non-cleavable pTyr mimic; Ki high-nM/low-µM ≈ substrate Km) [paper, Results]. Field: 600 and 800 MHz [Table 1]. pH 6.6/293 K is given only for literature kinetics (ref. 19); NMR-specific pH/temperature/buffer: not stated.

3. **Apo relative to** — The tyrosine-phosphorylated substrate, modeled by the non-hydrolyzable EGFR peptide Ac-DADE-X-LIP-NH2 [paper, Results].

4. **Verdict** — Strong. Apo-state CPMG/R1ρ dispersion in both enzymes resolves a minor closed-WPD-loop state whose shift signature is cross-validated against an apo-closed crystal structure (1SUG, via ShiftX2) and an independently measured peptide-bound spectrum, and whose closing rate tracks each enzyme's separately measured cleavage rate [paper, Table 1, Results].

5. **Major state** — Open WPD loop. PTP1B apo: pa = 97.5±1.4% [paper, Results]; matches "the many apo PTP1B crystal structures" (ref. 22, Barford et al. 1994); no single PDB ID given [paper]. YopH apo: pa = 97% [paper, Results]; open-state PDB ID not given [not stated].

6. **Alternative state(s)** — Closed, catalytically competent WPD loop. PTP1B apo: pb = 2.5% [paper, Results: "pa(pb) = 97.5 ± 1.4% (2.5%)"]; matches apo-closed 1SUG (1.95 Å apo PTP1B structure) [paper ref. 23; PDB 1SUG, data.rcsb.org]. YopH apo: pb ≈ 3% (100–pa, pa=97%) [our reading of the paper's data]; no closed apo-YopH PDB ID cited — illustrated generically via vanadate complex 2I42 (Fig. 1A,C) [paper, Fig. 1 legend].

7. **Evidence type** — ¹⁵N-CPMG dispersion (PTP1B apo and peptide-bound; YopH peptide-bound); TROSY off-resonance ¹H-R1ρ dispersion plus ¹⁵N TROSY Hahn-echo Rex for apo YopH, whose CPMG/¹⁵N-R1ρ curves were flat (kex ≥ 30,000 s⁻¹); direct shift-difference measurement (apo vs. bound) and ShiftX2 back-calculation from a crystal structure [Results; Table 1 footnotes].

8. **Exchange parameters** (rates/Δω: paper Table 1; pa/pb: paper Results text) — PTP1B apo: kex 920±190, kclose 22±5, kopen 890±190 s⁻¹, pb 2.5%; Δω 2.8–3.8 ppm (W179 2.8±0.9, F182 3.4±1.3, A189 3.8±0.6). PTP1B+peptide: kex 35±5, kclose 30±4, kopen 4.5±1 s⁻¹; Δω(A189) 3.2±0.3 ppm. YopH apo: kex 43,000±6200, kclose 1240 s⁻¹ (±200 in Table 1, ±280 in Results text — discrepant as printed), kopen 42,000±6000, pa 97%; Δωcalc A359 3.4±0.4, S361 6.4±1.2 ppm. YopH+peptide: kex 1790±240, kclose 1770±240, kopen 18±2 s⁻¹; pa 99%; Δω A359 3.5±0.4, S361 4.5±0.8 ppm.

9. **Residues or regions** — Ten-residue WPD loop. PTP1B: assigned W179, F182, G183, V184, S187, A189 (G183/S187 too weak, V184 overlapped; W179/F182/A189 analyzed); general acid D181. YopH: assigned T358, A359, V360, S361 (T358 overlapped, V360 low S/N; A359/S361 analyzed); general acid D356 [Results]. Nucleophile Cys215/Cys403, on the separate P-loop, which "does not undergo a significant conformational change during catalysis" [Results].

10. **Identity basis** — Three independent shift estimates converge for F182/A189, agree within error for W179: dispersion-fit Δω (apo), the measured apo-vs-peptide-bound shift difference (ΔδmeasN), and ShiftX2-calculated shifts for the closed conformer from 1SUG (ΔδcalcN) are "nearly identical" [paper, Results].

11. **Structural mapping and simulations** — PDB: 1SUG (apo, closed, human PTP1B, 1.95 Å; basis for Δδcalc) [ref. 23]; 3EB1 (PTP1B+inhibitor LZP-25, open despite binding) [ref. 27]; 2I42 (Y. enterocolitica YopH–vanadate, closed) and 3I80 (PTP1B, transition-state analog for hydrolysis, closed) [Fig. 1]. No new simulations run; prior MD (ref. 30) shows a faster (~ns) open-to-partly-closed motion matching Trp-fluorescence anisotropy, distinguished from the slower (~22 µs) full-loop transition here [Discussion].

12. **Functional relevance** — The closed loop positions the general acid (D181/D356) to protonate the leaving-group tyrosine oxygen during cleavage [Fig. 1D]. Apo kclose tracks each enzyme's independent kcleavage (PTP1B 22 vs. 30–80 s⁻¹; YopH 1240 vs. 1400–2000 s⁻¹, refs. 19–20) despite the enzymes' ~20-fold kcat difference [paper, Results: "YopH about 20-fold more active than PTP1B"]. Peptide binding leaves kclose unchanged but slows kopen ~200-fold (PTP1B), ~2300-fold (YopH), shifting equilibrium toward closed [Table 1: "peptide binding only slows loop opening"].

13. **Competing explanations addressed** — The faster (~ns) fluorescence/MD estimate for YopH reflects sidechain-only (Trp179) sensitivity vs. this paper's backbone-wide probe; a further ~50-fold gap vs. prior T-jump data reflects different ligands (peptide vs. p-nitrocatechol sulfate) [Discussion]. Tungstate (product mimic) release is identical for both enzymes, against differential release as the kcat-difference source [Results, Fig. S7].

14. **What this evidence cannot support** — Causal (not correlative) rate-limitation by loop closure: no general-acid or loop mutant was tested [inference from absence of such data]. That kclose alone accounts for kcat, also limited by a separate, slower hydrolysis step [paper]. Atomic structure of the minor closed state: not solved here; identity rests on shift agreement with static structures, and for YopH on two residues via an indirect Rex/Hahn-echo route [our reading of the paper's data]. Construct boundaries, mutations, NMR pH/temperature/buffer: not verifiable from the accessible text [not stated].
