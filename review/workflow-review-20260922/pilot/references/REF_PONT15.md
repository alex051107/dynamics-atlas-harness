# REF_PONT15 — sealed reference answer

Paper: Pontiggia F, Pachov DV, Clarkson MW, Villali J, Hagan MF, Pande VS, Kern D. Free energy landscape of activation in a signalling protein at atomic resolution. Nat Commun 2015;6:7284. DOI 10.1038/ncomms8284. Section locators follow the PMC author manuscript (PMC4470301, NIHMS684984); "Supplementary Fig." refers to its supplementary PDF.

## PART 1 — REFERENCE ENTRY

1. **Protein** — Nitrogen regulatory protein C (NtrC), N-terminal receiver domain (NtrC^R), a bacterial two-component response regulator [paper; Abstract, Introduction]. Species not stated in the paper; the deposited entries give *Salmonella typhimurium* [our reading of the paper's data; PDB 2MSL/2MSK entity]. UniProt not stated (PDB/SIFTS maps the chain to A0A0M3KKS6) [our reading of the paper's data; PDB 2MSL].

2. **Construct and conditions** — Wild-type receiver domain, residues 1–124, no mutations [our reading of the paper's data; PDB 2MSL/2MSK]. No nucleotide or cofactor. Apo sample: 1 mM ¹³C/¹⁵N protein, 50 mM sodium phosphate, 50 mM NaCl, no Mg²⁺; activated sample: 0.3 mM protein with 4.4 mM BeCl₂, 29 mM NaF, 7.2 mM MgCl₂ [our reading of the paper's data; PDB/BMRB sample records]. NMR at 35 °C [paper; Methods, "NMR structure refinement"], pH 6.75 [our reading of the paper's data; PDB 2MSL/2MSK]; 600 MHz (assignments) and 800 MHz cryoprobe (NOESY) spectrometers [paper; Methods]. The cited exchange data come from earlier 25 °C work [paper; Results, "NMR experiments support…"]. Simulations: CHARMM27 at 300 K (strings, MSM) and 320 K (Anton) [paper; Methods]; no phosphoryl group, BeF₃⁻ or Mg²⁺ is described in the simulated systems [inference; Methods].

3. **Apo relative to** — Phosphorylation of D54, mimicked in the NMR by BeF₃⁻ (the "BeF₃⁻ activated" form). Apo = unphosphorylated, BeF₃⁻-free NtrC^R [paper; Introduction; Fig. 5 legend].

4. **Verdict** — Strong: the paper reports that unphosphorylated NtrC^R interconverts between an inactive state and a ~15% active state at ~13,000 s⁻¹, and supports the active state with a BeF₃⁻ structure, an apo MSM whose ~100 μs process is comparable to that rate, and NOE markers; the exchange numbers are cited from refs 11–13, not measured here [paper; Introduction; Results].

5. **Major state** — Inactive (I): a heterogeneous ensemble; central α4 stable, termini partly helical, helicity extending partially to Q96; Y94 packed against α3 [paper; Results; Figs. 2F–I, 5A,D]. Population ≈85% [inference: complement of the cited ~15% active]. Structure: 2MSL (apo, 35 °C; deposited as "inactive state"), a population-averaged ensemble [paper; Accession codes; Fig. 5A].

6. **Alternative state(s)** — Active (A): structurally homogeneous; stable α4 rotated about its axis so that Q96 faces α3 and Y94 leaves it [paper; Results; Figs. 2B–E, 5E]. ≈15% of the apo protein [paper; Results, "NMR experiments support…", citing refs 12, 13]. Structure: mapped to 2MSK (BeF₃⁻-activated, 35 °C) and to the simulation active model; not determined in the apo protein [paper; Fig. 5B; Supplementary Fig. 5A]. The inactive sub-states (Fig. 2A) are simulation-derived parts of I, not separate alternative states [paper; Results].

7. **Evidence type** — No exchange experiment in this paper; the I/A exchange rests on cited "NMR dynamics data" (refs 11–13), and earlier 25 °C spectra showed exchange broadening [paper; Introduction; Results]. New evidence: NMR structures of the apo and BeF₃⁻ forms, chemical-shift helical propensity, and apo-versus-BeF₃⁻ NOE patterns [paper; Methods; Fig. 5]; MD: string method with umbrella sampling, Folding@home MSM, Anton runs [paper; Figs. 1–4]; MD-versus-measured S² comparison [our reading of the paper's data; Supplementary Fig. 5C].

8. **Exchange parameters** — Interconversion rate ≈13,000 s⁻¹ (kex or unidirectional: not stated) [paper; Introduction, citing refs 11–13]; active population ≈15% [paper; Results, citing refs 12, 13]; chemical-shift differences: not stated. Simulation estimates only: slowest MSM process ≈100 μs, imprecise [paper; Results; Supplementary Fig. 2]; I sub-states interconvert in a few μs, up to ~10 μs [paper; Discussion; "Comparison of MSM models…"]; two macrostates of ≈52% and 48% (not assigned to A or I); A = ~5% of the 2,168 microstates by count [paper; Results].

9. **Residues or regions** — Helix α4 (H84–Q96) and the α4–β5 loop, packing against α3; allosterically linked to D54 [paper; Results; Fig. 1A]. Switch side chains Y94 and Q96 (contacts to α3); also L87, S92, Q95, A98, F99, Y101, T82, K67 [paper; Figs. 1A, 2, 5C,E, 6]. F99 does not move between states in the new structures [paper; Results; Supplementary Fig. 5B].

10. **Identity basis** — The MSM's single slow process is comparable to the NMR I/A rate, so its two macrostates are taken as A and I [paper; Results, "The active and inactive states are kinetically defined"]; the BeF₃⁻ structure agrees with the simulation active model [paper; Fig. 5B; Supplementary Fig. 5A]; Y94–α3 NOEs (apo) versus Q96–α3 NOEs (BeF₃⁻) confirm the predicted α4-rotation marker [paper; Fig. 5E]. Experimentally, the apo minor state's identity with the activated state is inherited from refs 12–13 [inference].

11. **Structural mapping and simulations** — Previous inactive/active models (TMD end states, patched; PDB IDs not stated) and the new 2MSL/2MSK [paper; Fig. 1A; Methods]. Simulations sampled both states: strings Str1/Str2; 8,000 Folding@home runs (1.015 ms; 978 μs used), 2,168 microstates, 50 ns lag, PCCA into 2 or 10 macrostates [paper; Methods]; Anton ~21 μs from I and ~71 μs from A, with no A↔I transition [paper; Fig. 4; Supplementary Fig. 4]; MD-relaxed apo NMR models diverge [paper; Fig. 5F]; an independent Amber99SB MSM agrees qualitatively [paper; Fig. 6].

12. **Functional relevance** — Phosphorylation of D54 by NtrB stabilizes A, which propagates the signal to downstream partners and activates nitrogen-starvation genes [paper; Introduction, refs 13, 15]. Earlier H-bond mutants (marked S85D/G, Q96N, Y101F) raised the activation barrier without changing I or A [paper and our reading of Fig. 3F,G; ref 13]. No activity or binding assays here.

13. **Competing explanations addressed** — Non-converged strings as a sampling artifact: the unbiased MSM and committors show multiple pathways [paper; Fig. 3]. MSM bias from string starts: Anton end-state runs and an independent end-state MSM [paper; Figs. 4, 6]. I sub-states as transient intermediates: runs from one start commit to different I conformers with similar probability [paper; Supplementary Fig. 3D]. Old inactive-model errors (F99, α4 length): F99 flips late in Str2 and is not rate-limiting [paper; Results]. Different I heterogeneity between the two MSMs: attributed to CHARMM27 versus Amber99SB [paper; "Comparison…"]. T–Y coupling: Y101 moves independently of T82 [paper; Supplementary Fig. 3A,B].

14. **What this evidence cannot support** — A new exchange measurement, Δω, or populations at 35 °C; MSM populations or the 100 μs timescale as measured values; a solved structure of the apo minor state; experimentally resolved inactive sub-states; experimentally measured pathways or barrier contributions; generality to other proteins [paper; Results; Discussion; inference].

15. **Provenance** — DOI 10.1038/ncomms8284; PMC4470301 (author manuscript NIHMS684984); PMID 26073309. Figs. 1–6, Table 1 (NOE counts differ from Methods text), Supplementary Figs. 1–5. PDB 2MSL (apo/inactive) and 2MSK (BeF₃⁻/active) [paper; Accession codes]; BMRB 25125 (apo) and 25124 (BeF₃⁻), chemical shifts only, linked from the PDB entries; BMRB lists 273 K despite "35C" titles [our reading of the paper's data].

## PART 2 — MUST-GET-RIGHT

- **Apo means unphosphorylated NtrC^R, and the alternative apo state is the active conformer.** Apo is defined relative to D54 phosphorylation, with BeF₃⁻ as the NMR mimic. "Inactive" and "active" are two conformations that coexist in the unphosphorylated protein; phosphorylation shifts the equilibrium toward A. The terms do not mean "unphosphorylated protein" and "phosphorylated protein". [Introduction para. 2; Fig. 5 legend]
- **Verdict: strong, with the source of the exchange numbers flagged.** The ~13,000 s⁻¹ interconversion and the ~15% active population are cited from refs 11–13 (earlier NMR at 25 °C), not measured in this paper. A "weak" label is acceptable only if it names exactly this gap. "Candidate" or "not included" misreads the paper, which states the apo I/A equilibrium with a rate and a population. [Introduction; Results, "NMR experiments support…"]
- **Exchange numbers and what is missing.** ≈13,000 s⁻¹ and ≈15% A in apo, both cited. The paper has no chemical-shift differences, no CPMG/R1ρ/CEST/ZZ data and no population at 35 °C. [Introduction; Results, "NMR experiments support…"]
- **MSM numbers are simulation outputs, not measurements.** The slowest process is ≈100 μs, and the paper says a precise estimate is impossible because few transitions were sampled. The two macrostates are ≈52% and ≈48%, and the text does not say which is A. A holds ~5% of the 2,168 microstates by count, which is not a population. [Results, "The active and inactive states are kinetically defined" and "Homogeneous active versus heterogeneous inactive state"; Supplementary Fig. 2]
- **How NMR and simulations were combined.** The simulations came first (string, then the Folding@home MSM, then Anton) and produced predictions. The MSM macrostates were labelled A and I because their slow timescale is comparable to the NMR rate. New 35 °C NMR data (2MSL, 2MSK, chemical-shift helicity, NOEs) then tested the predictions, and the NMR models were relaxed by MD and compared with measured S². No exchange experiment was run. [Results; Fig. 5; Supplementary Fig. 5]
- **Distinguishing coordinate and residues.** The states differ in the rotation of helix α4 about its axis. Y94 contacts α3 in I, and Q96 contacts α3 in A (NOEs in the apo and BeF₃⁻ samples). In apo, α4 is partially helical up to Q96, whereas the old model ended it at Y94. F99 does not switch in the new structures. [Figs. 2C–I, 4E, 5C–E; Supplementary Fig. 5B]
- **Structures.** 2MSL is the apo structure, deposited as the inactive state. It is a population average over a sample that holds ~15% A. 2MSK is the BeF₃⁻-activated (active) structure. No structure of the apo minor state was solved; it is mapped to 2MSK and to the simulated A macrostate. [Accession codes; Fig. 5; Supplementary Fig. 5A]
- **I is heterogeneous, A is homogeneous, and both are defined kinetically.** I splits into sub-states that interconvert in a few μs (up to ~10 μs), and some I conformers lie within 2.5 Å backbone RMSD of A. This heterogeneity comes from the simulations and depends on the force field (CHARMM27 vs Amber99SB). [Figs. 2A, 4D, 6; Discussion]
- **Functional link.** Phosphorylation of D54 by NtrB stabilizes A, which propagates the signal downstream and activates nitrogen-starvation genes (refs 13, 15). Earlier H-bond mutants raised the activation barrier without changing I or A (ref 13). The paper runs no functional assay. [Introduction; Fig. 3 legend; "Nonnative H-bonds with multiple partners during transition"]
- **Construct and conditions.** Wild-type *S. typhimurium* NtrC residues 1–124 (from PDB). NMR at 35 °C, pH 6.75, 600 and 800 MHz. Simulations used CHARMM27 at 300 K (MSM) and 320 K (Anton) and model the unphosphorylated protein (inference: no phosphoryl group, BeF₃⁻ or Mg²⁺ is described). [Methods; PDB 2MSL/2MSK]

## PART 3 — OVER-CLAIMS

- **"This paper measured the I/A exchange (kex ≈13,000 s⁻¹, 15% active) by relaxation dispersion."** Both numbers are cited from refs 11–13 (earlier NMR at 25 °C). The paper runs no exchange experiment and gives no Δω.
- **"The MSM shows that ~5% (or ~48–52%) of apo NtrC^R is in the active state."** The 5% is a count of microstates. The 52/48 split is an MSM estimate from string-seeded runs and is not assigned to A or I. Neither is a measured population, and the paper does not reconcile either with the NMR value of 15%.
- **"The MSM gives the I→A rate (~10⁴ s⁻¹)" or "the MSM reproduces the NMR rate."** The paper says a precise estimate is impossible with so few rare transitions. The ~100 μs value is only "comparable" to the NMR rate.
- **"2MSK is the structure of the apo minor state" or "2MSL is a pure inactive structure."** 2MSK is the BeF₃⁻-activated protein. 2MSL is a restrained ensemble averaged over an apo sample that includes ~15% A, and it cannot resolve the I sub-states.
- **"NMR detected several alternative inactive sub-states."** The sub-states come from CHARMM27 simulations and are less heterogeneous with Amber99SB. The NMR support is indirect (partial helicity, NOEs, MD-relaxed models), and every sub-state belongs to I.
- **"Inactive means the unphosphorylated protein, and active means the phosphorylated protein."** Both states coexist in the unphosphorylated protein, and phosphorylation shifts their populations.
- **"Multiple pathways, non-native H-bonds and barrier lowering were shown experimentally" or "Anton captured A↔I transitions."** These results come from committor analysis and simulated trajectories. The Anton runs (21 μs and 71 μs) showed no A↔I transition, and Fig. 2J is a kinetic Monte Carlo meta-trajectory built from the MSM. The only experimental support is qualitative agreement with the ref 13 mutants.
- **"The two states have no structural difference" or "these features hold for all signalling proteins."** The rotation of α4 and the Y94/Q96 contacts to α3 distinguish the states. The claim of generality is the authors' speculation.
