## 1. PAPER
Hansen, A.L., Xiang, X., Yuan, C., Bruschweiler-Li, L. & Brüschweiler, R. "Excited-state observation of active K-Ras reveals differential structural dynamics of wild-type versus oncogenic G12D and G12C mutants." *Nat Struct Mol Biol* 30, 1446–1455 (2023). DOI 10.1038/s41594-023-01070-z.

Constructs: human K-Ras4B G-domain, residues 1–169, WT, G12C, G12D, each GTP- and GDP-bound (Methods p.11); GTP samples use native GTP, not an analog (p.1447). Conditions: 20 mM HEPES pH 7.0, 5 mM MgCl2, 5 mM BME, 5% D2O; all dynamics data (Table 1, Figs. 2–4) at 298 K, 850 MHz (+600 MHz for CPMG); assignment instead used 288 K (G12C·GTP)/283 K (WT, G12D·GTP), later transferred to 298 K (Methods p.11).

"Excited state" = state 1: a minor (~7–10%), ms-exchanging substate of the GTP-bound population (CPMG/CEST) — not a separate nucleotide-loading state (Table 1, p.1450).

Alternative-APO-state verdict: no, not in the nucleotide sense — K-Ras is GTP- or GDP-bound in every sample, never nucleotide-free (Methods p.11); GDP-bound protein itself showed no ms exchange (Suppl. Fig. 4, cited p.1452). It is more defensible under the Atlas's drug/effector-free definition: no drug or effector protein is in any sample, and state 1 is specifically the effector-incompetent substate (p.1449–1450). But that reading redefines "apo" as "effector-disengaged conformation of a nucleotide-loaded protein," conflating the nucleotide-occupancy axis with the drug/effector axis. Recommend a qualified tag, not a plain "apo" entry (my inference, not the paper's claim).

## 3. WORKFLOW TRACE
All EXPLICIT unless marked. (Switch I/II historically undetectable by X-ray/NMR — the starting gap, p.1446–1447.)
1. Constructs + sample engineering: subclone K-Ras4B(1–169) WT/G12C/G12D; tune buffer/temperature for native-GTP stability; randomize/interleave t1 acquisition to suppress hydrolysis noise (Methods p.11; p.1447).
2. Backbone assignment: 6 triple-resonance experiments + NOESY, NUS → >98% assigned incl. Switch I/II (p.1447; Methods p.11).
3. Global 2-state exchange fit: CPMG (850+600 MHz) + CEST (850 MHz), jointly fit in ChemEx → kex, p1, k21, k12 (Table 1, p.1450).
4. Per-residue Δϖ mapping: unsigned (CPMG)/signed (CEST) shifts localize to P-loop/Switch I/II/α3–β4 (Fig. 3a,b, p.1450).
5. Structural read of excited state: correlate shifts against GDP-bound and POTENCI random-coil references, by region → R²/RMSD (Table 2 p.1451; Fig. 3c–g p.1450).
6. Corroboration + control: NASR order parameters + SSP on ground states (Fig. 4, p.1448–1449, 1452) show the same regions flexible/disordered; standard model-free S2 (Suppl. Fig. 5) misses this, motivating NASR's use (p.1452).
7. Minimal X-ray ensemble for GDP ground state, fit to S2(NASR) → 6MBU 47%/4EPR 39%/6ASE 14% (Fig. 6, p.1452–1453; detail in Section 6d).
8. Cross-construct comparison + external check: WT>G12D>G12C on p1 and k21 (Table 1 p.1450); compare to literature GTPase turnover rates (refs. 34–35, p.1453). MD positioning: Section 7.

## 4. DETECTION & CHARACTERIZATION OF THE EXCITED STATE
15N/1HN CPMG (νCPMG 0–1,000 Hz) + 15N CEST (150 ms mixing, B1 in Suppl. Table 1); 22–50 nonproline residues/construct, Rex > 5 s⁻¹ (Table 1, p.1450). Global 2-state fit, ChemEx, bootstrap errors (p.1448–1450). kex=k21+k12: WT 400±12, G12C 326±11, G12D 301±17 s⁻¹; p1: WT 10.15±0.47%, G12D 9.00±0.44%, G12C 6.97±0.17% (Table 1, p.1450). Structural identity is inferred, not solved, by correlating excited-state Δϖ/Ω against Ω(GDP) and POTENCI Ω(random coil): Switch I vs. GDP R²=0.951/RMSD 3.05 ppm, vs. RC R²=0.875/2.10 ppm; Switch II vs. GDP R²=0.686/1.56 ppm, vs. RC R²=0.473/3.39 ppm; effector lobe (1–86) vs. GDP R²=0.881/2.15 ppm (Table 2, p.1451). Signed Δϖ (CEST) is required for these; CPMG alone gives only |Δϖ| (p.1451). Reading: excited state is GDP-like overall; Switch I approaches true random coil; Switch II fits GDP much better than random coil — structured-but-different, not fully unfolded (p.1448, 1452).

## 5. COMPETING EXPLANATIONS AND CONTROLS
Hydrolysis corrupting spectra: suppressed by randomized/interleaved acquisition, samples used ≤3 days (Methods p.11). Excited state as hydrolysis artifact rather than genuine substate: k21 (40.6 s⁻¹) stated far faster than kcat (<4×10⁻⁵ s⁻¹, refs. 34,35,42) — hydrolysis completes once per 10⁷ excited-state transitions, so the population isn't accumulated GDP product (p.1454). GDP-bound state showing similar ms exchange: checked, absent (Suppl. Fig. 4, p.1452). More than one excited state: single CEST dip per residue, no evidence for >1 substate (p.1451). Mutation-site Δϖ (G12X, G13) as dynamics vs. local chemistry: attributed to direct proximity to the mutation (p.1452). Global unfolding vs. localized exchange: not named explicitly, but Δϖ concentrates in P-loop/Switch I/II, small elsewhere (p.1448) — INFERRED as evidence against global unfolding.

## 6. STUCK POINTS
Explicit: (a) Switch I/II undetectable by prior NMR/crystallography; needed a new sample-prep/acquisition protocol before any dynamics analysis was possible (p.1447). (b) "Difficult to determine [structure] based on backbone 15N and 1HN chemical shift information alone" (p.1448) — resolved by comparing two reference models (GDP, random coil) instead of solving one directly. (c) The GDP-model correlation was imperfect (R²=0.88, effector lobe); outliers (T35, T58, G13, E37) were separately checked against γ-phosphate proximity (Suppl. Fig. 2) to attribute disagreement to nucleotide chemistry, not structure (p.1448, 1453). (d) No experimental ensemble exists for GDP-bound Switch II in solution; authors iteratively combined existing crystal structures, had to add the G12D structure (4EPR) to explain an S2(NASR) minimum, tested a 4th (4OBE) with no gain, kept a minimal 3-structure/47-39-14% ensemble (p.1452–1453) that still misses G60. (e) Standard model-free S2 missed the Switch dynamics; NASR (the group's own method) was needed instead (Suppl. Fig. 5, p.1452). (f) Mapping observed states onto the historical "state 2/state 1" names required an older, different-technique (31P NMR) equilibrium constant (ref. 6), not a direct measurement here (p.1449–1450).

## 7. ROLE OF MD AND STRUCTURES
No MD was run. All X-ray structures are pre-existing (5VQ2, 4OBE, 6MBU, 4EPR, 6ASA, 6ASE): one shows what a single static GTP structure misses (Fig. 1a); the rest form a population-weighted surrogate ensemble for the GDP ground state's own solution flexibility (Fig. 6), feeding the Switch I/II structural argument only indirectly. MD appears only in Discussion: prior K-Ras MD (ref. 49) found no WT-vs-mutant difference; kex is "too slow to be captured by traditional MD simulations"; this dataset is offered as a future MD benchmark, not used to run/validate MD here (p.1454). MD did not sample the transition in this study.

## 8. CLAIM AND LIMITS
Claim: K-Ras·GTP (WT, G12D, G12C) exchanges between an ordered, effector-competent ground state (2) and a minor, GDP-like, disordered excited state (1) at Switch I/II and the P-loop (Table 1 p.1450; Fig. 3 p.1450–1451). WT has both higher p1 and faster k21 than either mutant (values in Section 4); this parallels literature GTPase turnover rates (WT fastest, refs. 34–35, p.1453), used to argue differential excited-state accessibility contributes to WT-vs-mutant GTPase/effector differences — worded "may be instrumental" (p.1452) / "help rationalize" (p.1453), i.e. proposed, not proven. Stated limits: excited-state structure is inferred by correlation, not solved; the GDP-ensemble fit is imperfect at specific residues (p.1452–1453); MD has not reproduced this transition (p.1454).
