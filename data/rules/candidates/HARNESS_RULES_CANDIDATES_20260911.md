| **H1.** An external, structural verifier, not the model's own self-report or self-critique, is what catches an error; a self-evaluator or self-critique step without outside grounding is unreliable and can turn a correct answer into a wrong one. | verifier | M. Bran et al. 2024 (ChemCrow); Gou et al. 2023 (CRITIC); Huang et al. 2023; Kambhampati et al. 2024; Gottweis et al. 2026 | Bites failure #1, #2, #3, #8, #9, #11 (a checker that only confirms 'a number appears' caught 0 of 7 planted numeric errors; binding every number to its source operator result caught 6 of 7, per RESULTS.md); #15, #16 (same 0-vs-6-of-7 contrast, directly comparing an unbound checker to an operator-bound one); #25 (a rule already existed in the project's own table but was not applied structurally to a new definition). |
| **H2.** A self-correction result measured with foreknowledge of which answers are wrong (an oracle gate) overstates the model's real, unaided self-correction ability; only a correction gain measured without that foreknowledge should be read as genuine self-correction. | evaluation | Gou et al. 2023 (CRITIC*); Huang et al. 2023 | Literature only. |
| **H3.** Real execution against an external tool (a code interpreter, a search engine, a lab-automation API), not more careful wording of the prompt, is what fixes errors and cuts hallucination; a model that only imitates using a tool in text, without a real interpreter behind it, performs far worse than one with real execution. | tool | Boiko et al. 2023; M. Bran et al. 2024; Campbell et al. 2026; Gao et al. 2022; Gou et al. 2023; Yao et al. 2022 | Bites failure #23, #24 (submitted answers had a median of 2 Python calls per run and 4 zero-Python runs out of 80; a rule requiring a real operator call before submission is the only form shown to fix this). |
| **H5.** Access to the right tool/operator must be a structural requirement of the submission path, not something left to the model's own judgment of when a calculation is needed; an agent given only free-form access, with no forced tool call, fails at a materially higher rate than one whose path requires an operator result before it can answer. | tool | Kumar et al. 2026; Campbell et al. 2026; Skarlinski et al. 2024; Yao et al. 2022; Kambhampati et al. 2024 | Bites failure #23, #24, #31 (the free-analysis arm, with no forced operator call, produced zero usable numbers in 7 of 8 runs, citing read/call-length limits; the same question set on an operator-bound path reached 98-100% completion, per OPERATOR_PLAN_REPORT_ZH.md). |
| **H6.** Sensitivity to prompt wording and framing is model-specific, and can hide reliance on surface pattern-matching rather than genuine reasoning; a benchmark result obtained with one wording, on one model, does not transfer to a different wording or a different model without re-testing. | prompt | Campbell et al. 2026; Kambhampati et al. 2024; Gou et al. 2023; Huang et al. 2023; Gao et al. 2022; Yao et al. 2022 | Bites failure #4, #5 (a FRET-specific precondition, retrieved by method-family rather than by which computation the question actually needs, was applied to an NMR question); #18, #19 (pasting the full rule text or a method card into the prompt lowered scores rather than helping, and the method-card group's main discriminating question scored zero in every arm). |
| **H7.** A model-generated checkpoint, dialogue summary, or step-summary is not a verified substitute for the underlying raw data; treat it as unverified until it is checked against the source it claims to summarize. | tool | Campbell et al. 2026 | Bites failure #14 (admission-card files given to the agent were never read in 0 of 4 runs; only file names appeared in the tool log, a case of unread source material, the same failure class this rule targets). |
| **H8.** A results-only success metric conflates an output-formatting error with a real reasoning or analysis error, and rewards a right-final-answer-wrong-process solution; a step-level or rubric-based partial-credit check is needed to tell these apart. | evaluation | Lightman et al. 2023; Chen et al. 2024; Mitchener et al. 2025 | Bites failure #20 (a single yes/no rubric item scored zero in all three test arms and could not discriminate between them); #32 (an operator-bound arm still under-reported required parameter values, showing 'the operator ran' is not the same rubric item as 'every required value was reported'). |
| **H9.** Repeated sampling, majority voting, or multi-run consensus is a robustness check, not a correctness guarantee; it does not fix a systematically low-quality single-run analysis, and a low agreement rate across runs should be reported as low agreement, not resolved by adding more votes or by inventing a number when a comparison run is unavailable. | evaluation | Mitchener et al. 2025; Huang et al. 2023; Ghareeb et al. 2026; Gou et al. 2023 | Bites failure #29 (a missing second blind scorer was recorded as NOT_AVAILABLE rather than papered over with a fabricated agreement rate). |
| **H10.** A mid-pipeline processing choice (a post-processing window, a gating or clustering threshold) can change the reported physical result even when the underlying simulation or tool call ran correctly; such choices must be reported and justified, not left as silent defaults. | tool | Kumar et al. 2026; Ghareeb et al. 2026 | Bites failure #6 (a fixed 50 ns binning choice was described as giving an independent statistical unit while more than a quarter of the resulting bins actually disagreed with the trajectory's own labeled direction); #26 (an unstandardized 0.05 convergence threshold was compared to itself and was always zero); #27 (a single unbranched half-box-length distance check flagged real 62-70 A intramolecular distances as errors). |
| **H11.** An abstain/refusal option changes what a multiple-choice score means; removing it and seeing the score rise is not evidence of better reasoning by itself, it can mean the model fell back on memorized prior knowledge instead of the evidence it was given, so the evidence source behind a correct answer needs to be checked, and open-ended cross-validation is needed before a multiple-choice score is read as proof of real reasoning. | evaluation | Mitchener et al. 2025; Laurent et al. 2024 | Literature only. |
| **H12.** A private held-out data split, masked or perturbed test labels, and fresh out-of-distribution problems released after a model's training cutoff are needed before a passing score can be read as real capability rather than memorization or a shortcut; a string-match contamination check bounds but does not eliminate this risk. | evaluation | Laurent et al. 2024; Chen et al. 2024; Lightman et al. 2023; Gottweis et al. 2026 | Literature only. |
| **H13.** A generator or ranking agent's own output cannot serve as its own validation; role separation between a hypothesis/candidate-generating agent, a data-analysis or verification agent, and an external validator (a declarative checker or wet-lab result) must be kept, and a proxy ranking metric must not be read as the real objective it stands in for. | verifier | Ghareeb et al. 2026; Kambhampati et al. 2024; Boiko et al. 2023; Gottweis et al. 2026 | Literature only. |
