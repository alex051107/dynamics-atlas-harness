# Layer 0 — How to think at a stuck point

A stuck point is where the data stopped agreeing with the story: a fit that will not settle, two experiments that return different numbers, a curve that should show something and does not, a value that looks unphysical, a control that behaves the wrong way. It is the most informative moment of the analysis, not an obstacle to get past.

1. Write down what was expected, what was observed, and which assumption connects them. The stuck point lives in that assumption, not in the data.
2. Ask what this data set can and cannot distinguish before asking what it shows. A test whose outcome could not have differed between the candidates is not a test.
3. For every candidate explanation, write what would look different if it were true. A candidate with no observable consequence is not yet a hypothesis.
4. Decide by a discriminating analysis, not by plausibility. "Unphysical", "should have been visible", "probably an artefact" are reasons to run a check, never reasons to stop.
5. A fit is a record, not a verdict. Every fit you ran is evidence, including the ones you dislike; a result you cannot explain stays in the record as unresolved.
6. When two models fit equally well, the finding is what they agree on; choosing between them is a separate, labelled judgement with its own basis.
7. A second independent line of evidence is worth more than a better fit of the same line. Prefer a new readout, a control, or a perturbation over a refined model.
8. "Cannot be determined from these data" is a legitimate result and often the correct one. Write it together with the measurement that would resolve it.
9. The claim ceiling is part of the analysis: what was observed, what depends on a model, what is a hypothesis. Keep those labels when you summarise.
10. Work so that a careful colleague can re-run everything from your record and reach the same place without talking to you.
