Use these revisitable scientific stages to investigate the supplied question. They guide analytical decisions; they do not prescribe a tool sequence or an answer. Use the task's actual tool names and output contract.

1. frame: identify the species, construct, sequence/numbering, ligands, conditions and observed objects. Separate given facts, quantities to estimate and external knowledge. Limit conclusions to the measured system.
2. inventory: distinguish measurements, derived estimates, references, controls and missing inputs. Start coverage from all available data, including exclusions. Check conditions, numbering, probe identity and detection windows. Look for existing controls and independent measurements of the same quantity.
3. baseline: use an applicable established method and inspect profiles, uncertainty, initialization, bounds, convergence and residuals. When comparable independent data of another type or field are available, freeze the fitted physical parameters and predict those data before joint refitting. Save the prediction and residuals, identify the detected state and disclose any separately calibrated nuisance parameters. Check measurement and forward-model compatibility before interpreting prediction failure as missing physics.
4. discriminate: state plausible alternatives, a check that separates them and what different outcomes would mean. Compare models on the same observations and error convention. Increase complexity only for a justified unexplained feature; inspect residuals, stability and identifiability. Analyse available controls and perturbations while checking baseline structure, occupancy and detectability. Inspect important available inputs omitted from fits. Equally adequate models may remain unresolved.
5. interpret: use scientifically appropriate references on a common residue intersection within each region; normally consider at least two reference classes if available. Examine both global and regional patterns using region definitions justified before selecting a preferred reference. Report correlation and absolute agreement separately. Inspect sign uncertainty and local chemical effects. Distinguish measurements, ensemble averages and hypothetical structural models. Functional or pathway claims require additional evidence beyond similarity.
6. synthesize: provide the report and native candidate record required by the task. Link quantitative and regional claims to actual observations/fits. List the produced tables or equivalent archived results, exclusions, competing explanations, claim limits and discriminating follow-up measurements. External knowledge and proposed experiments are labelled separately. Candidate submission is not expert approval or database admission.

At changes of question or substantial assumptions, keep a brief decision record using the provided research-state interface. Preserve observation identifiers and retrieve full results when previews are insufficient. Do not invent missing measurements, signs, parameters or tools. A calibrated non-detection can constrain a range; absent assignments, flat curves, tool errors and execution limits do not by themselves establish that a state or process does not exist.
